**The Spiral SDLC Model is a risk-driven, iterative software development approach introduced by Barry Boehm in 1986, combining elements of waterfall, incremental, and prototyping models to manage risks through repeated cycles.**

## Overview

The Spiral Model emphasizes continuous risk assessment and iterative refinement. Each loop (iteration) represents a full cycle of planning, risk analysis, development, and evaluation. It is considered a *meta-model* because it integrates concepts from multiple SDLC models. GeeksforGeeks

## Four Quadrants / Phases

### 1. **Planning (Objective Setting)**

* Identify goals, constraints, and requirements.
* Explore possible solutions.
  GeeksforGeeks

### 2. **Risk Analysis**

* Identify risks related to cost, schedule, technology, and performance.
* Build prototypes to reduce uncertainty.
  GeeksforGeeks

### 3. **Development & Testing (Engineering)**

* Design, develop, and test selected features.
* Produce the next version of the product.
  teachingagile.com

### 4. **Evaluation & Planning Next Iteration**

* Customer reviews the current version.
* Feedback informs the next loop of the spiral.
  GeeksforGeeks

## Key Characteristics

* **Risk-driven**: Decisions are based on risk analysis at every stage.
* **Iterative**: Each loop improves the product.
* **Hybrid**: Combines waterfall structure with iterative prototyping.
* **Cost & Progress Visualization**: Radius = cost; angle = progress.
  GeeksforGeeks

## Advantages

* Strong risk management.
* Suitable for large, complex, high-risk projects.
* Allows evolving requirements.
* Early customer feedback.
  GeeksforGeeks

## Disadvantages

* Expensive and resource-intensive.
* Requires expert risk analysis.
* Can lead to indefinite looping without clear exit criteria.
  teachingagile.com

## Use Cases

* High-risk systems (e.g., aerospace, defense).
* Large enterprise applications.
* Projects with unclear or evolving requirements.
  teachingagile.com

The Spiral Model remains valuable for complex projects where risk management and iterative refinement are critical.
