The Agile software development is an iterative, flexible approach that delivers software in small increments, emphasizes collaboration, and is guided by the four core values of the 2001 Agile Manifesto: individuals and interactions, working software, customer collaboration, and responding to change.

Overview

Agile software development refers to a family of methods and practices rooted in the Agile Manifesto, created in 2001 by 17 software practitioners. It focuses on adaptability, customer involvement, and continuous improvement.

Core Values

According to the Agile Manifesto, Agile prioritizes:

* Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
* Working software over comprehensive documentation
* Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
* Responding to change over following a plan

Key Principles

Agile emphasizes:

* Iterative and incremental development
* Frequent feedback loops
* Close collaboration between cross-functional teams
* Continuous planning and learning

Agile Process

Typical Agile development cycles include:

1. Requirements gathering with customer involvement
2. Planning iterations or sprints
3. Development in small increments
4. Testing early and often
5. Deployment of working software
6. Maintenance and ongoing improvement

Popular Agile Frameworks

* Scrum – timeboxed sprints, roles like Product Owner and Scrum Master
* Kanban – continuous workflow and visual boards
* SAFe – scaling Agile across large organizations

Benefits

* Faster delivery of value
* Better adaptability to changing requirements
* Improved team collaboration
* Continuous customer feedback

Challenges

* Requires strong stakeholder involvement
* Needs disciplined backlog management
* Can fail without proper training or team focus

Agile remains one of the most widely adopted approaches in modern software development due to its flexibility and customer-centric nature.
