The Iterative SDLC model is a cyclical software development approach where the system is built in repeated iterations—each including planning, design, development, and testing—producing an improved working version every cycle. It is best suited for projects with evolving requirements and emphasizes continuous feedback and refinement.

Overview

The Iterative SDLC model breaks a project into smaller iterations. Each iteration delivers a functional increment of the software, which is reviewed and refined based on stakeholder feedback. This allows early detection of issues and adaptation to changing requirements.

Key Characteristics

Cyclical development: Repeated build–test–refine cycles.

Functional output each iteration: Each cycle produces a usable version.

Continuous feedback: Stakeholders review each iteration.

Flexibility: Easily adapts to evolving requirements.

Risk reduction: Issues identified early in the process.

Phases in Each Iteration

Planning & Requirements – Define goals and scope for the iteration.

Design – Create architecture or component design.

Development – Build the planned features.

Testing & Evaluation – Validate functionality and gather feedback.

Advantages

Early delivery of working software.

Better risk management through early detection.

High flexibility and adaptability to change.

Continuous user involvement improves alignment with needs.

Disadvantages

Can be more complex to manage due to multiple iterations.

Potentially higher cost from repeated changes.

Requires disciplined planning and documentation.

When to Use

Requirements are unclear or expected to evolve.

Projects needing early prototypes or user feedback.

Complex systems requiring gradual refinement.

The iterative SDLC model remains widely used today because it supports continuous improvement and aligns well with modern, feedback-driven development environments.
